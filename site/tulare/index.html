Tulare works harder than almost any town its size in America. It's the dairy capital of the country, home to the World Ag Expo, and the roofs here prove it: milk and cheese processing plants running washdown cycles around the clock, refrigerated warehouses holding product against 100-degree afternoons, metal ag buildings and equipment dealerships lining Highway 99, and trucking terminals that move the Valley's output north and south every day. These are not decorative roofs. They protect production lines, cold chains, inventory, and audit files — and when one fails, the cost is measured in a lot more than drywall.

Allstate Spray Foam Roofing serves Tulare from our Tulare County shop, under 20 minutes away. This is home turf — not a market we drive into, but the county our family business has worked for more than 20 years. We're fully licensed (CA Lic #C-2-1052735), BBB accredited, and members of the Spray Polyurethane Foam Alliance, and we've built our trade around the roof systems this town's building stock actually needs: spray polyurethane foam, 100% silicone coatings, flat and metal roof repair, and closed-cell commercial insulation.

Why foam in Tulare specifically? Because the physics fit. Spray foam roofing is seamless — no laps, seams, or exposed fasteners for washdown humidity to work through from below or winter ponding to attack from above. It insulates at roughly R-6.5 per inch while it waterproofs, which matters enormously in a town where so many roofs sit over refrigeration and every degree of heat gain is compressor runtime purchased at commercial rates. And its silicone topcoat reflects the South Valley's punishing sun — summers here clear 100°F for weeks at a stretch, and UV exposure ranks among the harshest in the country — while shrugging off the standing water that tule-fog winters leave sitting on flat roofs for weeks because nothing evaporates between storms.

The building stock seals the argument. Tulare's commercial roofs are overwhelmingly flat and low-slope: tilt-up distribution boxes in the industrial parks, pre-engineered metal buildings at the dealerships and dairies, older masonry blocks downtown carrying two or three generations of built-up roofing, packinghouses and cold storage out toward Lindsay. Foam and silicone were made for exactly these roofs — applied over what's there (usually no tear-off), building slope into chronic ponding areas, encapsulating the thousands of fasteners that make metal roofs leak, and adding the insulation these buildings were built without. For dairy and food-processing facilities, the fit is even tighter: seamless surfaces with no fastener corrosion in chemical-laden rooftop air, condensation control over washdown humidity, and crews accustomed to working over live production with the containment discipline and documentation that USDA-adjacent audits expect.

Our services in Tulare cover the full envelope. New spray foam roof systems install at $4–$8 per square foot and turn an aging flat or metal roof into a seamless, insulated, reflective system. Silicone roof coatings restore sound roofs at $2–$4 per square foot — the renewable maintenance cycle that means a well-kept foam roof never faces a tear-off. Our repair crews handle flat, foam, and metal roof leaks with drone-and-core diagnostics that find the real entry point instead of caulking the stain. And our commercial insulation practice puts closed-cell foam into the metal shops, warehouses, cold storage, and processing facilities that fight this climate from the inside — killing the summer oven effect and the fog-season condensation drip in one application.

Every engagement starts the same way, free: a drone survey of the entire roof, core sampling where the assembly's history warrants it, and a written bid in your hands within 48 hours. No pressure theater, no replacement-first sales script — the evidence decides whether your roof needs a repair, a restoration, or a new system, and we show you the imagery either way. The work itself is backed by industry-leading leak-proof guarantees and by the more practical guarantee of proximity: the crew that installed your roof stays twenty minutes away for the life of it.

We schedule around Tulare's calendar because we live on it. February belongs to the World Ag Expo at the International Agri-Center — if your building serves the show or sits near the grounds, fall is the time to call so the roof is done before the gates open. Processors book us against maintenance shutdowns; ag-adjacent sites get staging planned around harvest dust and truck traffic; winter work gets slotted into the dry windows between fog events. And our radius covers the whole southern county: down Highway 99 through the dairy belt at Tipton and Pixley, east on 137 to Lindsay's packinghouse district, and out Highway 190 to Porterville's commercial and industrial stock.

If you own or manage a building in Tulare — a plant, a warehouse, a dealership, a downtown block — the next step is one phone call: (559) 739-9519. We'll put a drone over the roof, tell you the truth about what's up there, and hand you a number you can plan around within 48 hours.
