What "100% Silicone" Means and Why It Matters

Not all roof coatings are close to equal. Acrylic coatings are water-based, cheaper, and genuinely useful in the right spot — but they re-emulsify under standing water and erode faster under UV. High-solids 100% silicone is a different animal: it is moisture-cure chemistry that is unaffected by ponding water, essentially immune to UV degradation, and stays flexible through Fresno's 100°F-plus summers and foggy near-freezing winters alike. On a flat roof in this climate — where drainage is rarely perfect and the sun is never merciful — silicone is the coating we specify most, because it is the one that is still performing in year fifteen. When we quote a silicone flat roof coating for a Fresno building, we quote high-solids 100% silicone at the manufacturer's specified millage, not a bargain blend stretched thin.

Millage and Granules: The Two Specs That Decide How Long It Lasts

Two specification lines separate a fifteen-year coating from a five-year one, and both are invisible once the roof is white. The first is dry-film thickness — coating performance and coating warranties are written in mils, and a system applied thin fails early no matter whose bucket it came from. We measure thickness during application rather than estimating by the bucket count, and the specified millage appears on your written bid where you can hold us to it. The second is granule embedment: broadcasting ceramic granules into the wet topcoat produces a tougher, higher-traction surface that resists foot traffic, hail bruising, and bird pecking, and it moderates silicone's two honest weaknesses — slickness when wet and dirt pickup. On Fresno roofs with regular HVAC service traffic, we recommend granules as a matter of course; the modest cost difference buys years of wear.

The Fresno Conditions a Roof Coating Has to Survive

Consider what a coated roof endures here in a single year. From June through September, surface temperatures cycle from warm mornings to a scorching afternoon peak day after day — the thermal expansion and contraction that cracks brittle coatings. UV exposure in the south Valley is among the most intense in the country, chalking and thinning lesser products year by year. Autumn drops ag dust from harvest across every horizontal surface in the county, clogging scuppers so winter water sits in ponds. Then tule fog season keeps the roof wet for days at a stretch, probing every pinhole while acrylics soften. A 100% silicone membrane is the only coating class that treats all of that as a normal Tuesday: ponded water cannot re-emulsify it, UV cannot meaningfully degrade it, and it stays elastic through every thermal cycle. That is why silicone dominates our Fresno recoat work from Blackstone Avenue retail to Malaga warehouses.

There is an energy dimension to all this heat as well. A fresh white silicone surface reflects the large majority of the solar energy that a weathered dark roof absorbs, which means the deck under it runs dramatically cooler on a 105°F afternoon and the rooftop HVAC equipment stops fighting a radiant oven all summer. California's Title 24 cool-roof requirements for low-slope re-roofs point in the same direction, and a silicone restoration typically satisfies them without any additional scope. For most Fresno owners the coating decision is made on leak protection and replacement avoidance — the summer utility relief and the code compliance arrive as bonuses in the same bucket.

Silicone Roof Coating Pros and Cons

No product is perfect, and you should hear the cons from your contractor, not from the internet after you sign. The honest ledger:

  • Pro — ponding immunity: silicone is the only mainstream coating warrantied under standing water, which matters enormously on Fresno's older low-slope decks.
  • Pro — UV permanence: silicone does not chalk away in Valley sun the way acrylics do; its reflectivity fades slowly with dirt, not with degradation.
  • Pro — seamless and renewable: it flashes every detail monolithically, and when it finally wears, you clean and recoat directly over it — no future tear-off.
  • Pro — cost and speed: $2–$4 per square foot and days, not weeks, of installation with the building fully operating.
  • Con — slick when wet: cured silicone is slippery under fog-drip or rain; roofs with regular foot traffic get walk pads at hatches and service routes.
  • Con — dirt pickup: silicone holds dust, and Fresno has plenty; reflectivity benefits from an occasional rinse. Granule-embedded surfaces reduce this.
  • Con — adhesion is prep-critical: silicone bonds only to a properly cleaned, sound, dry substrate — and only new silicone sticks to old silicone. Prep discipline is most of the job.
  • Con — not a structural fix: coating does not repair a saturated roof. If core samples come back wet, those sections get addressed first or the coating is wasted money — we will say so.

Weigh that ledger against Fresno's specific conditions and the pros compound while the cons stay manageable. Slickness is solved with walk pads and granules; dirt pickup costs a little reflectivity, not waterproofing; prep discipline is a contractor-selection problem, not a product problem. Meanwhile the ponding immunity and UV permanence address exactly the two failure modes that end most Valley flat roofs. That asymmetry — cons you can engineer around versus pros that target your actual climate — is why silicone has become the default restoration spec across the region's commercial stock, and why the buildings that tried bargain acrylic on ponding decks a decade ago are calling us now.

Recoating Foam Roofs: The Maintenance Cycle That Makes SPF Immortal

If your building already has a spray foam roof, silicone recoating is not an option — it is the scheduled event the whole system is designed around. The foam provides the insulation and the seamless waterproofing; the coating is the sacrificial UV shield that wears in its place. In Fresno sun, that shield lasts roughly 10–20 years depending on product and millage, and each $2–$4 per square foot recoat resets it completely without touching the foam beneath. Owners who keep that cycle never buy another roof; owners who skip it let UV reach the foam itself, and then the repair scope grows. The aging math is laid out in how long does spray foam roofing last, and the underlying system is explained in our spray polyurethane foam roofing guide. Metal Roof Coating for the Metro's Ag and Industrial Buildings

Metal roof coating is its own discipline, and the Fresno area — with its packing sheds around Sanger and Selma, shop buildings out toward Kerman, and older industrial metal in Calwa and Malaga — has no shortage of candidates. Aging metal roofs fail at fasteners and lap seams: thousands of them, each backing out or rusting as panels cycle through the Valley's daily temperature swing. Our metal restoration process treats rust, seals every fastener head individually, bridges the lap seams with reinforcement, and then floods the whole field with silicone into one seamless membrane. The result stops existing leaks, prevents the next generation of them, and drops summer surface temperatures dramatically — metal buildings feel the reflective difference more than any other type because so many of them are barely insulated. Where a metal building also needs real R-value, we pair coating work with foam — see the metal retrofit section of our Fresno spray foam roofing service.

Owners of metal buildings often ask whether coating is worth doing on a roof that is already rust-streaked, and the answer depends on what the rust means. Surface oxidation on panels that are still structurally sound is exactly what restoration is for — it gets treated, primed, and sealed under the membrane, and the corrosion clock stops because water and oxygen no longer reach the steel. Panels rusted through, or purlin-level structural issues, are a different conversation, and the drone survey plus a hands-on walk tells us which building you have before anyone quotes anything. What we will not do is spray a bright white coat over failing steel to buy two quiet years — a coating should be the end of your leak story, not an intermission.

Buildings and Corridors Where We Coat

A sampling of where silicone work takes our crews in the metro: retail strips along Blackstone and Shaw with twenty-year-old built-up roofs that pond behind parapets; downtown Fresno offices where tear-off logistics over occupied space make restoration the only sane option; distribution and cold storage boxes in south Fresno and Malaga where owners want the reflectivity gain as much as the leak protection; Clovis flex-commercial along the CA-168 corridor hitting its first recoat window; and metal ag buildings from Kingsburg to Kerman with fastener leaks in the hundreds. The building types differ; the physics — UV, heat, dust, fog, imperfect drainage — are the same everywhere in Fresno County, and silicone answers all of them.

The substrates vary as much as the neighborhoods, and each gets its own prep protocol:

  • Aged spray foam — the natural recoat candidate; cleaned, spot-repaired, and recoated directly. This is the maintenance cycle SPF is designed around.
  • Built-up and modified bitumen — Fresno's most common older commercial roofs; asphalt bleed and chalk demand thorough washing and the right primer, but restored BUR is some of the best-value work we do.
  • Smooth single-ply (TPO/PVC/EPDM) — aging membranes with sound attachment can often be coated rather than torn off; adhesion testing during the assessment gives the yes or no.
  • Metal panels — fastener-by-fastener sealing plus seam reinforcement before the flood coat; the most transformation per dollar of any substrate we touch.
  • Previously coated roofs — old silicone takes new silicone after cleaning; old acrylic over-coats with silicone once chalk is washed off and adhesion is verified.

How a Coating Project Runs, Start to Finish

First the free assessment: drone survey, core samples where the deck's moisture history is unclear, written bid in 48 hours. Then preparation, which is most of the labor: pressure-washing the field (that ag-dust film again), drying, cutting out and patching failed areas, sealing seams and fasteners, and priming where the substrate demands it. Only then does silicone go down, sprayed or rolled in measured passes to the specified thickness, with detail work at every curb, drain, and penetration done by hand. We verify millage as we go — coating warranties are written in mils, and thin spots are where leaks are born. Most Fresno projects finish in days, the building operating underneath the whole time. Weather discipline matters here as much as on foam work: we do not coat wet steel or fog-damp decks, because adhesion is everything.

Timing a Coating Project Around the Fresno Calendar

The Valley gives coating crews a generous but not unlimited season. Late spring through early fall offers the long dry windows where production runs fastest — though peak-summer afternoons ask for early starts so material behaves on a hot deck. The window to respect is tule fog season: from roughly late November into February, decks can stay damp past midday, and moisture trapped under fresh coating is a blister factory. We schedule winter work around the fog rather than through it, and we tell owners plainly when a December call should become a January project. The practical advice: if your roof showed wear this summer, book the assessment now rather than after the first winter leak — the calendar is kindest to owners who plan one season ahead, and the free drone survey costs nothing to schedule.

Restore or Replace? The Question the Assessment Answers

The honest boundary: silicone restoration is for roofs that are aged but fundamentally sound — dry insulation, attached membrane, workable drainage. It is not for roofs whose insulation is waterlogged or whose substrate is failing; coating those is disguising a problem you will pay for twice. That is why our assessment includes core sampling and not just a walk-around. Roughly speaking, when a roof's wet area stays small, restoration wins economically by a wide margin; when saturation is widespread, we will show you the cores and talk about foam or replacement instead. Either way you get the evidence, the numbers, and a written recommendation — and the decision stays yours.

There is also a middle path worth knowing about: when a roof is too far gone for coating alone but the owner wants the seamless, renewable endgame, a new spray foam system over the existing deck delivers both — fresh insulation and waterproofing at $4–$8 per square foot, finished with the same silicone surface and entering the same 10–20 year recoat cycle. Plenty of our coating assessments end with that recommendation instead, and the same 48-hour written bid covers whichever scope the cores justify. The point of doing the diagnostics before the proposal is that you are choosing between real options priced from real evidence, not between a salesman's good-better-best sheet.
