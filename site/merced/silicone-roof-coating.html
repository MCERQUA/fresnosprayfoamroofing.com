What "100% Silicone" Means and Why It Matters Here

Not all roof coatings are the same chemistry, and in Merced County the difference is not academic. Acrylic coatings are water-based; they're cheaper, but they re-emulsify under standing water — and standing water is a fact of life on Valley flat roofs, where minimal slope meets winter storm cells and weeks of tule fog that never let ponds dry. High-solids silicone is moisture-cured and genuinely hydrophobic: it can sit under a pond indefinitely without softening, peeling, or losing adhesion. It is also the most UV-stable coating chemistry in common use, which matters when your roof spends June through September under a sun that pushes air temperatures past 100°F and surface temperatures far higher. When we say we install 100% silicone systems, we mean high-solids silicone applied at specified thickness — not a bargain blend, not an acrylic with silicone in the marketing. On Merced roofs, chemistry is destiny.

How Silicone Restoration Works, Step by Step

A coating is only as good as what's under it, so the process starts with proof, not paint. First, assessment: drone survey of the entire roof, moisture inspection, and core sampling where the roof's history is uncertain — if there's saturated insulation under your membrane, we find it now, cut it out, and replace it, because coating over trapped water is how restoration projects fail. Second, cleaning: pressure washing to strip the Valley's signature load of ag dust, almond-harvest grit, and diesel film off the surface — adhesion lives and dies on this step. Third, repairs: seams reinforced, splits and punctures patched, ponding areas addressed, penetrations re-flashed, rusted metal treated and primed. Fourth, application: high-solids silicone applied by spray or roller at the specified millage — thickness is measured, not eyeballed, because millage is the difference between a 10-year and a 20-year system. Fifth, verification: wet-film readings during application, a final inspection with photo documentation, and a written record of the system installed. The whole sequence usually takes days, not weeks, and your building stays occupied and operating throughout — no tear-off, no exposed deck, no dumpster train through the parking lot.

Silicone Over Spray Foam: The Renewable Roof

If your building already has a spray polyurethane foam roof — and plenty of Valley buildings foamed over the past forty years do — silicone recoating is not an option, it's the design. SPF systems are engineered to be recoated every 10 to 20 years; the coating sacrifices itself to the sun so the foam never has to. Recoat on schedule and the same foam stays in service for decades — this is the renewability that makes spray polyurethane foam roofing the lowest lifecycle-cost system in the Valley. Miss the window, though, and UV starts eating exposed foam surface, turning a $2-to-$4 recoat into a scarify-and-refoam project. If you've bought or inherited a Merced building with a foam roof of unknown age, that's reason enough for a free assessment: ten minutes of drone footage and a millage check tell us whether you're due, overdue, or fine for years. Silicone Roof Coating Pros and Cons — Straight Answers

We sell this system, and we'll still give you both columns honestly, because a coating in the wrong application fails and takes our name with it.

The pros. Ponding-water immunity — silicone is the only mainstream coating that shrugs at standing water, which on minimal-slope Valley roofs is disqualifying for the alternatives. Best-in-class UV stability under a Central Valley sun that logs more than 3,000 hours of annual sunshine. Reflectivity that drops roof surface temperature by up to 60–80°F on a 100-degree day, with real cooling-load savings and easy Title 24 cool-roof compliance. No tear-off: the existing roof stays as substrate, landfill fees stay unspent, and the building never sits open. Speed and quiet — days on the roof, no demolition, business as usual below. Renewability — when the coating ages, you clean and recoat again; the roof becomes a maintained asset instead of a periodic catastrophe. And price: $2 to $4 per square foot against $4 to $8 for new systems.

The cons. Silicone is slick when wet — walkable areas get granulated surfacing, and we detail walkpads at service equipment. Once silicone is down, only silicone sticks to it; you're committed to the chemistry for future recoats (a fair trade, but a real one). It holds dirt visibly more than acrylics, and under Merced's ag-dust load the bright white will mellow — reflectivity declines a little as it does, and an occasional wash restores most of it. It is a restoration system, not a resurrection system: a roof with widespread saturated insulation or a failing deck needs repair or replacement first, and our core samples will say so in writing. And thin-mil bargain jobs fail — millage discipline is everything, which is why we document wet-film thickness as we go. Weigh the columns and the conclusion for most structurally-sound Merced flat roofs is lopsided, but you should get to see both columns before you sign anything.

Coating Over TPO, Built-Up, and Modified Bitumen

Single-ply membranes age in a known arc: the sheet chalks, seams get brittle, and somewhere past year fifteen the warranty conversation turns into a replacement conversation. But a TPO or PVC roof that is aging is not the same as a roof that is failing — and a silicone flat roof coating over a prepared, adhesion-primed single-ply routinely resets the clock for a decade or more at a quarter of replacement cost. The prep is substrate-specific: single-ply gets cleaned aggressively and primed for adhesion, seams get reinforced with mesh or sealant, and any punctured or wet areas get cut out and patched before a drop of coating goes down. Built-up and modified bitumen roofs — the standard on downtown Merced's older commercial blocks and plenty of county buildings — take silicone well too, once blisters are addressed and the surface is stabilized; the coating locks down alligatored surfaces that would otherwise keep shedding gravel into the drains every storm. The disqualifier is always the same and it's always underneath: saturated insulation. That's what our core samples are for, and it's why our restoration bids hold up while cheaper drive-by quotes turn into change orders.

The Energy Math on a White Roof in Climate Zone 12

Merced sits in CEC climate zone 12, where cooling drives the commercial energy bill for most of the year. An aged, dark flat roof on a July afternoon can run 150–170°F; a fresh high-reflectance silicone surface on the same afternoon runs dramatically cooler — commonly 60–80°F closer to air temperature. That difference conducts straight into your cooling load, your rooftop-unit runtime, and — for Livingston's refrigerated processors — compressor duty cycles that never get a season off. The reflective surface also extends the roof's own life, because nearly every aging mechanism on a roof runs on heat. On the compliance side, California's Title 24 sets aged-reflectance and emittance minimums for low-slope nonresidential re-roofing, and CRRC-rated 100% silicone systems clear them cleanly — we include the product documentation with your permit package. We won't promise a specific percentage off your utility bill, because honest contractors don't guess at your building's load profile; we will say that the direction is always down, the effect is largest on under-insulated metal and single-story buildings, and the owners who feel it most are the ones running conditioned or refrigerated space under big Valley roofs.

Ponding Water: The Chronic Disease Silicone Actually Tolerates

Every flat roof in Merced County ponds somewhere — around settled drains, along the sagging mid-span of a fifty-year-old structure at Castle, behind parapets where the original slope was optimistic. Winter makes it worse: storm water lands, tule fog keeps it from evaporating, and the same pools sit for weeks. Ponding is where acrylic coatings die and where seamed membranes get probed relentlessly at their weakest points. Silicone's defining trait is that it does not care — it cures into a waterproof solid that maintains adhesion under standing water indefinitely, which is why it is the only coating chemistry we will put on a minimal-slope Valley roof. That said, tolerance is not endorsement: chronic ponds still load the structure and collect the ag-dust sludge that clogs drains, so our restoration scope usually includes drainage improvements — pond fills with foam or leveling compound, drain and scupper service, added overflow paths — so the coated roof sheds water the way the original design intended. Coating plus drainage correction, done together, is what turns a problem roof into a quiet one.

Timing a Coating Project Around Merced's Weather

Silicone is moisture-cured, which makes it forgiving, but application still wants a clean weather window: dry surface, temperatures in range, and no dew flash before skin-over. In practice that means Merced's long dry season — April into November — is prime coating season, and we schedule winter work around tule-fog burn-off and storm gaps. Summer work starts at first light to beat the afternoon heat. This is also why waiting until the first November leak to think about your roof is expensive: the best restoration window is the dry months before the storm season that will test it. Get assessed in spring or summer, coat on schedule, and let the first atmospheric river of the season hit a brand-new membrane. Buildings on the Highway 140 Yosemite corridor with summer-peak businesses get the inverse plan — we coat in the shoulder seasons so crews aren't overhead during your revenue months.

Silicone Roof Coating Price: What Moves the Number

Within the $2-to-$4 range, five factors set your exact price. Size — bigger roofs cost less per square foot, and Castle-scale roofs benefit most. Condition — a roof needing extensive seam work, saturated-insulation removal, or heavy rust treatment carries more prep cost than one that needs washing and touch-ups; this is most of the spread. Substrate — coating over existing foam is typically the least prep-intensive; metal with thousands of fasteners carries detail-labor hours; single-ply needs adhesion priming. Millage spec — a 20-year system carries more material than a 10-year system, priced accordingly and documented. Access and logistics — a single-story warehouse with drive-up access prices better than a downtown Merced block over occupied storefronts. Your written bid itemizes all five, and because we survey by drone and core-sample before quoting, the number on the paper is the number on the invoice — no discovery surcharges three days into the job.

Life After the Coating: Keeping the System Healthy

A restored roof is a low-maintenance roof, not a no-maintenance roof, and the habits that protect it are cheap. Twice a year — once after almond harvest dusts the county in late summer, once after the first big storms — walk the roof or have it walked: clear the drains and scuppers, sweep debris off the field, and look at the detail work around every penetration. After any trade works on the roof — HVAC techs, solar installers, sign hangers — check where they walked and what they screwed down, because service punctures are the leading cause of leaks on otherwise healthy coated roofs, and a silicone repair caught early is a ten-minute patch. Keep the documentation we hand you at closeout — millage readings, photos, product data — with the building's records; it matters at sale, refinance, and warranty time. And when the coating begins to show honest wear a decade or two out, the renewal is a wash-and-recoat, not a project: that recoat-forever cycle is the entire economic point of the system, covered in depth in how long spray foam roofing lasts. Owners who want it handled entirely can ask about scheduled maintenance visits when we write the bid — for multi-building owners at Castle or along the 99 corridor, one standing arrangement covers the whole portfolio.
