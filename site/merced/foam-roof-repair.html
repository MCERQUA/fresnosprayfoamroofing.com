The Leaks Merced Roofs Actually Spring

Twenty years of Valley repair calls sort themselves into patterns. Ponding failures: minimal-slope roofs settle, drains sit high, and winter water stands for weeks under tule fog that never lets it evaporate — until it finds a seam. Seam and flashing failures: built-up roofs on downtown Merced's older blocks alligator and split; single-ply membranes on 99-corridor warehouses let go at welds and at the base of parapets; every roof-to-wall transition is a suspect. Fastener failures: the county's enormous inventory of metal roofs — ag shops, pre-engineered steel buildings, the Air Force-era structures at Castle — leaks at screw heads whose neoprene washers the sun destroyed years ago. Penetration failures: every pipe, curb, vent, and rooftop unit is a hole somebody flashed once, decades ago. Service damage: the HVAC tech's dropped tool, the solar crew's unsealed standoffs, the new conduit run screwed straight through the membrane. And on existing foam roofs — of which the Valley has plenty — coating wear that exposed foam to UV, plus the occasional bird-pecked or hail-dinged spot, all of it eminently patchable if it's caught before water saturates beneath.

Flat Roof Repair: How We Diagnose Before We Touch Anything

Foam Roof Repair Done by People Who Install Foam

If your building wears a spray polyurethane foam roof, hire foam people to repair it — general roofers reach for asphalt mastic, which is the wrong answer on SPF and creates adhesion problems for the next honest repair. Foam repair done right is clean and permanent: damaged or UV-degraded foam gets cut back to sound material, the area is dried and prepped, new foam or a compatible sealant fills the void flush, and the patch is coated with silicone matched to the field. Because SPF is fully adhered and closed-cell, damage stays local — water in a gouge doesn't spread laterally under the membrane the way it does under loose-laid systems — so a foam roof that gets timely repairs effectively doesn't age structurally; the whole story is in our guide to how long spray foam roofing lasts. We repair SPF roofs we didn't install all the time, including orphaned roofs whose original contractor is long gone. And when a foam roof's coating is worn across the whole field rather than in spots, the right repair is actually a recoat — $2 to $4 per square foot and the roof is renewed for another 10 to 20 years, a path covered in depth on our spray foam roofing page.

Leak Repair on Metal Roofs: Fasteners, Laps, and Rust

Metal buildings deserve their own playbook because Merced County has square miles of them. The classic metal leak inventory: backed-out or washer-failed fasteners (thousands per roof, each a candidate), open lap seams where thermal cycling has worked panels apart, rust-through at low spots and around old repairs, ridge and eave closures gone brittle, and skylight or vent curbs flashed with whatever was on the truck in 1988. Repair scope runs from surgical — replacing and encapsulating fasteners, sealing laps with reinforced silicone detail work, converting and priming rust — up to systemic, where the honest answer is that the roof has ten thousand failure points and needs a full silicone coating system or a foam-over to end the game of whack-a-mole. We price both paths in the same written bid so the choice is yours, made with real numbers. For the hangar-scale metal at Castle Commerce Center, that surgical-versus-systemic decision is often the most consequential facilities call the site manager will make this decade, and it deserves core samples and a drone map, not a guess from the parking lot.

Ponding Water: Repairing the Roof's Geometry

Some leaks aren't a hole — they're a shape. Water that stands more than 48 hours after a storm is attacking your roof full-time: magnifying UV, breeding algae, loading structure, and probing every seam it touches, all winter long under fog that keeps evaporation near zero. Real ponding repair fixes the geometry, not just the symptom. Depending on the roof, that means foam pond fills that rebuild slope toward drains (a signature SPF capability — sprayed foam builds elevation exactly where needed), drain and scupper service or additions, crickets behind curbs and rooftop units, and overflow paths where parapet roofs currently rely on a single drain and hope. On badly settled structures — common on older county buildings — we'll tell you plainly what slope correction can and can't achieve. Every ponding repair we do gets documented with before-and-after drone shots so you can see the water paths change.

While You Wait: Stopgaps That Help and "Fixes" That Hurt

Water is coming through the ceiling and the crew is days out — what should your maintenance team actually do? First, protect what's below: move or tarp inventory and equipment, catch water in containers, and if drips are near electrical, kill the circuit and call your electrician before anything else. Second, manage the water on top if the roof is safely walkable: clearing a clogged drain or scupper is the single highest-value emergency action on a flat roof, because most "sudden" interior floods are really a blocked drain turning the roof into a swimming pool. Third, temporary patching — done modestly: on a visible split or open seam, clean and dry the immediate area as best you can and apply a compatible sealant or peel-and-stick patch as a bridge, and photograph everything first for your records. What not to do: don't smear asphalt mastic across a foam or silicone roof (it contaminates the surface and complicates the permanent repair), don't screw anything through the membrane, don't pressure-wash toward a suspected entry point, and don't send anyone onto a wet, slick, or fog-soaked roof for any reason — no patch is worth a fall. Cold Storage and Food-Plant Repairs: A Different Standard

Roof leak repair over a refrigerated or food-processing space is its own discipline, and Livingston-area operators know why. A slow leak over cold storage doesn't just stain a ceiling — it saturates insulation and quietly raises refrigeration load for months; in freezer spaces it builds ice inside the roof assembly, and the damage compounds invisibly until an energy bill or a core sample exposes it. Food-safety rules add constraints: repairs must be scheduled against sanitation windows and production calendars, materials and debris control matter more, and nobody can have a repair crew improvising over an open processing line. We plan those jobs with the plant manager — timing, access, containment, documentation — and our core-sampling discipline earns its keep here more than anywhere, because on refrigerated buildings the difference between dry and saturated insulation is worth real money every operating day. If your facility runs cold rooms, don't wait for a visible drip; the assessment is free, and thermal patterns plus cores will tell us what the roof is hiding.

Repair or Replace? How We Make the Call Honestly

Some roofs should be repaired; some repairs are just expensive procrastination. Our rule of thumb after twenty years: if the deck is sound, the insulation is dry (cores prove it), and failures are localized, repair — and expect years of additional service. If saturation is spreading, if you're paying for leak calls every season, or if repair quotes are stacking toward a third of restoration cost, stop patching: a silicone restoration at $2–$4 per square foot or a new SPF system at $4–$8 resets the whole clock and usually costs less over five years than serial emergency repairs plus interior damage. Because Allstate does all three — repair, restoration, and full SPF installation — we have no incentive to steer you to the big ticket. The written assessment prices the paths side by side and tells you which one we'd choose if the building were ours. Merced's Repair Calendar: Fog Season Is the Deadline

The Valley gives roof owners a long, generous repair season and then punishes anyone who wastes it. April through October is dry and predictable — every repair, coating, and foam scope is schedulable. November flips the switch: the first Pacific storms arrive, tule fog settles in between them, and the roof that "just needs looking at someday" starts dripping onto pallets in Livingston or classroom-adjacent retail near UC Merced. Fog season is uniquely hard on marginal roofs because nothing dries — a seam that weeps in a storm normally gets a drying interval; under fog it stays wet for weeks and keeps feeding the insulation below. Our strong advice to every Merced County owner: get the assessment in the dry months, do the repairs before Halloween, and spend the winter not thinking about your roof. We schedule north-Valley repair batches weekly all year, but fall slots fill first — the callers in January are the ones who meant to call in September.
