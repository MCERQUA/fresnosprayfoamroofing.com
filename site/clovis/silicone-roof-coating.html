Silicone Roof Coating in Clovis: The Case for Restoration

Silicone roof coating gives Clovis building owners a third option between patching a tired roof forever and paying for a tear-off it may not need. Most flat roofs in eastern Fresno County do not fail structurally — they weather. The membrane surface chalks and craze-cracks under one of the highest UV loads in the country, seams and flashings open under summer heat cycling, and by year fifteen the roof leaks even though 90 percent of the assembly underneath is still sound. A 100% silicone coating system addresses exactly that situation: the existing roof is cleaned, repaired at its failure points, and sealed under a seamless, UV-immune, waterproof membrane at $2–$4 per square foot — typically a fraction of what replacement costs once demolition, disposal, and new material are counted. Allstate Spray Foam Roofing has been coating Central Valley roofs for more than twenty years as a family-owned, fully licensed contractor (Lic #C-2-1052735), BBB accredited and an SPFA member, with crews reaching Clovis from our Tulare County shop in about 55 minutes.

The timing question is the one that decides most coating projects, and it deserves plain language. A roof is a restoration candidate while its bones are still good — sound deck, mostly dry insulation, failures concentrated at seams, flashings, and the weathered surface. Wait too long and the window closes: water saturates the insulation field, the deck starts moving, and the only honest options left are sectional rebuilds or full replacement at several times the price. The frustrating part of this trade is meeting owners two winters after the coating window shut, holding repair invoices that add up to half a restoration and a roof that now needs the expensive answer anyway. The free assessment exists to put a date on your roof's window. A drone survey and a handful of core samples tell us — and you — whether this is a $2-per-foot year, a $4-per-foot year, or a year to budget for something bigger, and the written bid arrives within 48 hours either way.

What a 100% Silicone Membrane Actually Does

Not all coatings are close cousins, and the distinction matters in this climate. Acrylic coatings are water-based; they re-emulsify under standing water and wear thin under UV, which makes them a poor match for Valley roofs that pond through the winter and bake through the summer. Asphaltic coatings dry out and crack on the same cycle that killed the original roof. High-solids 100% silicone is a different chemistry: it is moisture-cure, meaning humidity helps rather than hurts it; it is permanently UV-stable, so Fresno County's 260-plus sunny days cannot chalk it away; and it tolerates ponding water indefinitely — the single property that separates it from nearly everything else in the coating aisle. Applied at proper millage over a prepared surface, it forms a seamless rubber-like membrane that moves with the roof through 100-degree heat swings without splitting, and its bright white surface reflects the solar load that used to pour through the deck into your conditioned space.

Application quality is where identical materials produce opposite outcomes, so the specification matters as much as the chemistry. A proper system is applied in measured millage — typically two passes on a first-time restoration — with wet-film thickness checked during application rather than guessed afterward. Details are done first and by hand: seams get reinforcement where movement demands it, fasteners get individually capped on metal roofs, and flashings get brush-grade material worked into the transition before any field coating flows. Walk paths and equipment surrounds get granules embedded into the final coat, solving silicone's one genuine handling drawback — its slickness when wet — exactly where people actually walk. Every one of those line items appears in our written bid, because a coating quote that only states a price and a color is hiding the variables that determine whether the membrane lasts eight years or eighteen.

Silicone Flat Roof Coating Over Every Common Substrate

Silicone's versatility across substrates is what makes it the workhorse of roof restoration. Over spray polyurethane foam, silicone is the standard protective layer — every SPF roof carries a coating, and recoating on schedule is what makes a foam roof effectively permanent (the full system story is in our spray polyurethane foam roofing guide). Over single-ply membranes like TPO and EPDM, silicone seals the seams and flashings that are the membrane's chronic weak points, buying a decade or more from a roof whose field material is still serviceable. Over modified bitumen and built-up roofs — the dominant stock on Clovis's older commercial buildings — silicone locks down alligatored, oxidized surfaces before they crack through. And over metal panels, covered in depth below, it solves the fastener-and-seam leak pattern that plagues every aging steel roof in the Valley. The free assessment tells us which situation your roof is in: drone survey of the field, core sampling where saturation is suspected, written bid within 48 hours.

One substrate note saves Clovis owners real money: mixed roofs. Older commercial buildings that have been added onto over the decades — common in Old Town and along the original arterials — often carry two or three different roof systems meeting at awkward transitions, and those transitions are reliably where the leaks live. Because silicone adheres across all of the common substrates, a single restoration can unify a mixed roof under one continuous membrane, eliminating the transition details that no patching program ever permanently solves. It is one of the quiet places where a coating outperforms even a partial replacement, which would simply create a new transition to leak.

Pros and Cons — the Honest Version

We sell coatings nearly every week, and we still tell owners the whole truth about them, because a coating applied to the wrong roof fails and takes our name with it. The genuine advantages: silicone costs a fraction of replacement at $2–$4 per square foot; there is no tear-off, no landfill load, and minimal disruption; it handles ponding water and UV better than any competing chemistry; the white surface cuts summer cooling loads; and it is renewable — when it weathers thin in 10 to 20 years, you recoat again rather than replace. The honest limitations: silicone is slick when wet, so designated walk paths get granule embedment; it holds dirt and dulls over time, which affects looks more than performance but is worth knowing; once a roof is siliconed, future work must also be silicone, because almost nothing else adheres to it; and — most important — a coating is a restoration, not a resurrection. A roof with widespread saturated insulation, rotten decking, or structural issues needs those areas cut out and rebuilt, or needs a new roof entirely, and our core samples will say so before we quote a coating that should not happen.

Metal Roof Coating: Fasteners, Seams, and Rust

Metal roofs earn their own section because Clovis's satellite economy runs on them — the packing and processing buildings around Sanger, the shop buildings out toward Friant, the contractor yards and light-industrial steel of the Clovis Industrial Park. An aging metal roof leaks in a completely predictable pattern: thousands of fastener gaskets dry-rot on the same schedule, panel laps loosen as the steel oil-cans through daily heat cycles, and rust establishes at every scratch and low spot. Coating that roof is a system, not a paint job. We wash the panels, treat rust, detail every fastener head and lap seam with brush-grade silicone or seam tape, then flood-coat the field to specification. The result seals all several thousand penetrations at once, stops the leak chase for good, and drops the panel temperature dramatically in summer — steel that ran too hot to touch in July reflects most of that load instead. For an occupied shop or warehouse, it is the difference between a working roof and a re-roofing project the business cannot schedule.

Ponding Water and the Clovis Winter

Clovis gets nearly all of its ten-to-twelve inches of annual rain between November and March, often in concentrated storm systems coming up the Valley — which means a flat roof spends the wet season being tested precisely where it is weakest. Ponding is the classic failure accelerant: water collects in deflected bays and around clogged drains, and whatever coating or membrane sits under that pond degrades fastest. Two facts matter here. First, 100% silicone is the one mainstream coating chemistry that genuinely tolerates permanent ponding — it will not re-emulsify or blister under standing water the way acrylics do. Second, tolerance is not an excuse for bad drainage: during prep we clear and resettle drains, and where ponds are deep or structural, we quote foam build-up to restore slope rather than coating over a lake and calling it solved. Between storms, tule fog keeps roofs damp for days at a time; silicone's moisture-cure chemistry shrugs at that, but the prep windows matter, which is one more reason coating season in the Valley is best scheduled before the fog settles in.

Drainage stewardship continues after we leave, and we are direct with owners about their half of the bargain. A coated roof sheds water beautifully, but no membrane can drain through a scupper packed with cottonwood leaves or a drain basket buried in harvest dust. The maintenance ask is small — clear the drains before the first November system, glance at them after each big storm, keep rooftop trades from leaving debris — and it protects both the roof and the guarantee behind it. Where a building has a history of drain problems, we say so in the assessment and, where the structure allows, add overflow protection to the scope, because the cheapest insurance against a ceiling collapse is a second way off the roof for water.

The Recoat Cycle: How 10 to 20 Years Becomes Forever

The most misunderstood number in this business is coating life. A silicone membrane weathers gradually — it does not fail on a birthday. Depending on millage, sun exposure, and foot traffic, the coating serves 10 to 20 years before it thins to the point of needing renewal, and renewal is the whole point: clean the surface, repair any incidental damage, apply new silicone, and the clock resets at $2–$4 per square foot. On a foam roof, this cycle is what makes the system effectively permanent — the foam never sees daylight, so it never degrades, and only the sacrificial coating layer is ever replaced. Owners who internalize this stop thinking about their roof as a depreciating asset with a demolition date and start budgeting it like scheduled maintenance, which is exactly what it becomes. The long-run math is laid out in how long spray foam roofing lasts, and it is the strongest argument for getting a restorable roof under coating before it deteriorates past the restoration threshold.

Between recoats, the system asks very little, but a light inspection rhythm pays for itself. We recommend a roof check twice a year — once after the storm season ends in spring, once before it begins in fall — plus a look after any rooftop trade work, because HVAC and solar crews cause more coating damage in this Valley than weather does. Small punctures and cuts caught early are trivial silicone repairs; the same damage left through a winter becomes a wet-insulation cut-out. Owners who want it handled entirely can put the building on our inspection calendar, and either way the drone makes the survey fast, thorough, and documented.

How We Coat a Roof in Clovis

Every coating project follows the same disciplined arc. It starts with the free assessment — drone survey of the entire field, moisture readings and core samples where the story is uncertain, and a written bid within 48 hours that specifies substrate condition, repair scope, and coating millage. Project day one is preparation: pressure washing, drain clearing, cutting out and drying any saturated sections found in the survey. Detail work follows — every seam, fastener, flashing, curb, and penetration gets hand-treatment before a drop of field coating goes down, because coatings fail at details, not in the field. Then the flood coat is applied to specification, with granule-embedded walk paths at rooftop equipment and access routes. We close with a final inspection, documented millage readings, and maintenance guidance — mostly keeping drains clear through the November-to-March storm season and calling us rather than a handyman if rooftop trades cut into the membrane. Buildings stay occupied throughout; silicone application is low-odor and staged around your operating hours where needed.

Season and weather get managed, not wished away. Silicone's moisture-cure chemistry gives it more scheduling tolerance than most coatings, but application still wants a clean, dry surface and a rain-free cure window — which in practice makes late spring through mid-fall the natural Clovis coating season and makes September the smartest month on the calendar: past the worst heat, ahead of the first storms, with the roof's summer damage fully visible to the survey. Fog-season work is possible on the right roof with the right windows, but we would rather tell you to wait ninety days than apply a membrane into conditions that shortchange its cure.

Can you coat over my old flat roof instead of replacing it?add

Usually, yes — if the roof is fundamentally sound. Silicone restores built-up, modified bitumen, single-ply, foam, and metal roofs. How does silicone handle ponding water in winter?add

Better than any other mainstream coating. 100% silicone tolerates permanent ponding without re-emulsifying or blistering, which matters in Clovis where nearly all annual rain lands between November and March. Will a white silicone roof actually lower my cooling costs?add

In this climate, yes, measurably. How long does a silicone coating last before recoating?add
