Spray Foam Roofing Built for Clovis Heat, Fog, and Growth

Spray foam roofing in Clovis solves the two problems that kill flat roofs in the eastern Fresno County basin: seams and heat. Every conventional low-slope system — built-up gravel, modified bitumen, TPO, PVC — is assembled from pieces, and every piece has an edge. Those edges are where thermal movement concentrates, and Clovis delivers extraordinary thermal movement: rooftop surface temperatures north of 160°F on a July afternoon along Shaw Avenue, followed by a 40-degree overnight drop, repeated a hundred times a summer. Then winter arrives and tule fog keeps the same roof damp for days at a time, working moisture into every fastener hole and lap seam that summer opened up. Spray polyurethane foam sidesteps the whole failure sequence because it has no seams to open. The foam is applied as a liquid that expands and bonds monolithically to the substrate, flowing around penetrations, up parapet walls, and over curbs as a single continuous membrane. There is no edge for the heat to work on and no lap for the fog to soak.

How Spray Polyurethane Foam Actually Works

SPF is a two-component chemistry — an isocyanate and a polyol resin — combined at the spray gun under heat and pressure. The mixture reacts within seconds, expanding roughly thirtyfold and curing into rigid closed-cell foam that bonds tenaciously to concrete, metal, wood, and most existing roof membranes. Three properties make it a roof rather than just an insulation layer. First, the closed-cell structure is itself waterproof; the millions of sealed cells do not wick or absorb water the way fibrous insulation does. Second, the foam is fully adhered across every square inch, so there is no path for water to travel laterally beneath it — the failure mode that makes single-ply leaks so maddening to trace. Third, the foam is self-flashing: it expands around pipes, drains, HVAC curbs, skylights, and parapet transitions, which is exactly where 90 percent of conventional roof leaks originate.

Over the cured foam we apply a protective elastomeric coating — typically 100% silicone — with granules embedded where foot traffic warrants. The coating shields the foam from UV, takes the weathering hit, and is the renewable layer in the recoat cycle. The result is a roof assembly with no fasteners, no seams, no flashing details cut by hand, and no thermal bridges through the insulation. The Central Valley Climate Test

Roofing systems get sold on lab specs, but they fail in the field, and the eastern Fresno County field is uniquely demanding. Consider what a Clovis roof endures in a single year. Summer brings 40-plus days at or above 100°F, rooftop membrane temperatures that can exceed 160°F, and one of the highest annual UV doses in the country. Fall drops ag dust from harvest across every horizontal surface — fine sediment that clogs drains and holds moisture against membranes. Winter parks tule fog over the Valley floor for days, keeping roofs continuously damp between the storms that deliver most of the year's ten-to-twelve inches of rain in a three-month window. Spring winds coming down the Sierra foothills through the CA-168 corridor pry at loose edges and lift anything mechanically fastened.

SPF answers each stress directly. The foam is fully adhered, so wind uplift has no purchase point — a genuine consideration for buildings in the open northeast growth corridors. The silicone topcoat is UV-stable and does not chalk away in the sun the way acrylics do, and it sheds the winter damp without absorbing it. Ponding water, the chronic disease of aging Valley flat roofs, is often engineered out during application: because foam is applied as an expanding liquid, our installers build slope and crickets into low areas, restoring positive drainage that the original roof lost decades ago. No membrane system can do that without a full tapered-insulation redesign.

Reflectivity carries the summer half of the equation. A weathered dark roof in Clovis absorbs solar energy all afternoon and re-radiates it into the building well into the evening — which is why rooftop units keep running at 9 p.m. in August. The white silicone surface over foam reflects the majority of that load, and the foam beneath blocks what the coating does not reflect. Maintenance in this climate is refreshingly modest, but it is not zero, and we say so: fall drain clearing before the storm season, a rinse when harvest dust builds up enough to dull the reflective surface, and a periodic inspection so small coating wear gets touched up years before it matters. Owners who give a foam roof that minimal attention get the full recoat interval out of it; owners who ignore every roof equally at least start from a system with no seams to neglect.

Insulation Value and Title 24

California's Title 24 energy code makes roof performance a regulatory matter, not just a utility-bill matter, and reroofing projects can trigger insulation and cool-roof requirements. SPF is one of the cleanest compliance paths available for low-slope commercial work: the foam adds continuous insulation at roughly R-6.5 per inch with zero thermal bridging, and the white silicone topcoat delivers the high solar reflectance that cool-roof provisions call for. Instead of engineering rigid-board insulation, cover boards, and a membrane as separate layers, a single applied system handles insulation, waterproofing, and reflectivity together. We document foam thickness and coating specifications so your project file supports the permit, and because the insulation is on the exterior of the deck, conditioned space gains the benefit without losing an inch of interior clearance — something warehouse operators in the industrial park care about more than any R-value table.

There is a planning angle worth flagging for owners weighing timing. Reroofing projects that trigger Title 24 provisions can turn a simple membrane swap into an insulation upgrade project, with the cost stacking accordingly. SPF collapses that stack: the same application that waterproofs the roof supplies the continuous insulation and the reflective surface in one specification. For buildings changing hands, changing tenants, or heading into HVAC replacement — all common moments when energy documentation gets scrutinized in a growing market like Clovis — a foam roof puts the envelope numbers on the right side of the file before anyone asks for them.

Metal Building Roofing: Foam Over Aging Panels

Clovis and its satellites — Sanger's packing sheds, Friant's shop buildings, the contractor yards off Fowler and Sunnyside — run on metal buildings, and metal roofs have a predictable end-game: the fastener gaskets dry out, the panel laps loosen, rust blooms at the scratches, and every one of several thousand screw penetrations becomes a potential leak. Replacing panels is expensive and disruptive; screwing down a second skin adds thousands of new holes. Foam takes a different route entirely. Applied directly over the cleaned and primed panels, SPF encapsulates every fastener and lap seam in one monolithic layer, stops the oil-canning movement that backs screws out, kills the condensation drip that plagues uninsulated steel, and converts an R-nothing metal skin into an insulated roof assembly. The building gets quieter in the rain and dramatically cooler in July at the same time. For metal building owners weighing panel replacement against foam, the drone survey and core-sample assessment settles the question with data rather than guesswork.

Foam Against the Alternatives

Building owners comparing bids in the Fresno-Clovis market usually see SPF quoted against TPO, and the comparison deserves straight talk. TPO is a fine membrane installed by a fine crew — but it is a seamed, mechanically fastened system, and every seam is welded by hand on your roof, in Valley conditions, at whatever pace the schedule allows. Its insulation is a separate board layer bridged by thousands of fasteners, each one a small thermal short-circuit. SPF has no seams to weld and no fasteners to bridge; insulation and waterproofing are the same monolithic layer. TPO also cannot correct ponding without a tapered redesign, while foam builds drainage during application. Where TPO wins is a low upfront bid on a simple roof, and we say so when it is true.
