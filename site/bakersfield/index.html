Bakersfield is the hardest place in our entire service area to own a flat roof. The Kern County seat sits at the hot, dry southern end of the San Joaquin Valley, where summer temperatures grind past 100°F for weeks at a stretch, UV exposure is relentless, annual rainfall barely clears six inches, and the air carries a steady load of ag dust from the surrounding citrus, carrot, and almond operations plus grit from the oil patch. Every one of those conditions attacks a low-slope roof in a different way — heat bakes the plasticizers out of single-ply membranes, thermal cycling opens seams and flashings, UV chalks and embrittles exposed surfaces, and dust settles into ponding areas where the first winter rain turns it into a leak-finding paste. Allstate Spray Foam Roofing has spent more than twenty years solving exactly these problems for building owners across the southern valley, and Bakersfield is where our seamless, self-flashing, insulating roof systems earn their keep faster than anywhere else.

We are a family-owned company, fully licensed (CA Lic #C-2-1052735), BBB accredited, and a member of the Spray Polyurethane Foam Alliance. Our crews dispatch from our Tulare County shop, about ninety minutes north of Bakersfield straight down CA-99. That drive matters less than you might think: we batch Bakersfield-area assessments weekly, and when a project is ready, our installation rigs mobilize fully self-contained — foam, coating, compressors, and generators on the truck — so there is no staging delay, no rented yard space, and no dependence on local suppliers to keep a job moving. Whether the building is a distribution center off Highway 99, an oilfield service shop out Highway 58, a cold storage plant in Shafter, or a strip retail center on White Lane, we show up ready to work and we leave behind a monolithic roof with real insulation value.

Bakersfield's building stock plays directly to the strengths of spray polyurethane foam and silicone coatings. The metro has grown into one of California's most important logistics hubs — the junction of Highway 99 and Highway 58 funnels freight between the coast, the valley, and the Mojave, and the big-box distribution centers that followed have millions of square feet of low-slope roof between them. The energy sector keeps thousands of metal-roofed shops, pipe yards, and field offices working around Oildale and east Kern. Ag processors handling citrus, carrots, and almonds run refrigerated and conditioned space where every point of roof R-value shows up on the utility bill. All of these buildings share the same math: enormous flat or low-slope roof areas, punishing cooling loads, and owners who cannot afford to shut down operations for a tear-off. Spray foam roofing solves that equation with roughly R-6.5 of insulation per inch, applied directly over most existing roofs, finished with a reflective, UV-stable coating — and renewable with a fresh coat every 10 to 20 years instead of a landfill-bound replacement.

Our four core services cover the full life of a Bakersfield flat roof. Spray foam roofing gives new and existing buildings a seamless insulated membrane at $4–$8 per square foot installed. Silicone roof coating restores aging foam, metal, and smooth-surface roofs for $2–$4 per square foot — a fraction of replacement cost, with none of the disruption. Our roof repair service handles leaks, ponding damage, split seams, and storm damage on foam and flat roofs across the metro, from downtown Bakersfield out to Delano, Shafter, Wasco, and Arvin. And our commercial spray foam insulation service brings the same closed-cell chemistry inside — metal building walls and ceilings, cold storage envelopes, warehouse roof decks — where Bakersfield's heat makes air sealing and R-value worth real money under California's Title 24 energy code.

Every engagement starts the same way: a free assessment. We fly the roof with a drone, pull core samples where the roof's history is unclear, and put a written bid in your hands within 48 hours — no vague verbal estimates, no pressure. If a $3,000 repair and a maintenance plan will carry your roof another five years, that is what the bid will say. If the roof is at the end of its life, we will show you the photos and the core samples that prove it. That straight-answer approach is how a Tulare County family company earns work in a market as competitive as Bakersfield, and it is why so much of our Kern County work comes from owners who manage more than one building.
